# Key Texts

## Source anchors

- Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185
- Sahih al-Bukhari 2014
- Sahih al-Bukhari 38
- Sahih Muslim 1161

Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185 frames Ramadan with guidance, ease, and gratitude. Allah commands fasting for those who witness the month, while also making room for illness and travel with compensating days later. Even at the level of obligation, the ayah reminds the believer that Allah intends ease and not hardship.

The Sunnah adds the spiritual promise around the month. In Sahih al-Bukhari 2014, fasting Ramadan with faith and hope of reward is linked to forgiveness. In Sahih al-Bukhari 38, standing in prayer in Ramadan with the same faith and hope is also linked to forgiveness. Sahih Muslim 1161 points the believer toward the last ten nights in the search for Laylat al-Qadr.

## Further Detail

These texts should be read as a reliable starting point, not as an attempt to cover every legal branch or scholarly discussion. Fasting trains the body and heart together. The foundation is taqwa: restraint from lawful things for Allah so the servant can become stronger against unlawful desires and heedlessness.

## Practice Notes

The practical goal is to connect hunger to remembrance, mercy, and self-command. A fast that teaches patience should also soften the heart toward the needy and make obedience easier after Ramadan ends. If a detail would need a disputed ruling or a long comparison to explain fairly, it belongs outside this beginner guide.
